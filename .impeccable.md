## Design Context

### Users
Chess players aged 13+ who want to quickly find an opponent, join public or private clubs, and compete in club-run knockout or IPL-style tournaments. The primary jobs are starting a game without friction, understanding whose turn it is, and following tournament progress.

### Brand Personality
Focused, competitive, and premium. The product should feel like a serious ranked gaming platform built around chess: fast, tactical, and credible without becoming noisy or juvenile.

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark competitive-arena styling: charcoal surfaces, warm white type, disciplined vermilion actions, and muted olive status accents. Use condensed display typography, compact data labels, hard edges, and restrained angular cuts. Avoid cartoon pieces, neon sci-fi effects, glassmorphism, and decorative gradients.

### Design Principles
- Keep the chessboard and current match state visually dominant.
- Make primary actions obvious while keeping secondary controls quiet.
- Use asymmetry, typographic hierarchy, and compact competitive data like a professional match client.
- Adapt the board, bracket, and navigation for mobile rather than hiding features.
- Meet WCAG AA contrast, visible focus, keyboard operation, and reduced-motion preferences.
