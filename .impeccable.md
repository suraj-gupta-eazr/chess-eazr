## Design Context

### Users
Chess players aged 13+ who want to quickly find an opponent, join public or private clubs, and compete in club-run knockout or IPL-style tournaments. The primary jobs are starting a game without friction, understanding whose turn it is, and following tournament progress.

### Brand Personality
Focused, competitive, and welcoming. The product should feel like a well-run local chess club with the clarity of a printed tournament sheet, not a noisy gaming platform.

### Aesthetic Direction
Light-first editorial chess-club styling: warm ivory surfaces, near-black ink, muted board greens, and oxblood red for decisive actions and tournament highlights. Use a distinctive serif display face with a clean humanist sans-serif body. Avoid neon gaming aesthetics, glassmorphism, generic dashboard card grids, and decorative gradients.

### Design Principles
- Keep the chessboard and current match state visually dominant.
- Make primary actions obvious while keeping secondary controls quiet.
- Use asymmetry, typographic hierarchy, and rules like a tournament programme.
- Adapt the board, bracket, and navigation for mobile rather than hiding features.
- Meet WCAG AA contrast, visible focus, keyboard operation, and reduced-motion preferences.
